# Fact-check, October 2026

All 120 works were checked against the holding museums' records first (collection pages and open APIs: the Met, V&A, Art Institute of Chicago, Tate's open data, Centre Pompidou, Musée d'Orsay, Belvedere, Prado and others), then museum and academic publications, with Wikipedia used only as a pointer. Every hosted image was checked against its Commons file page to confirm it shows the version named on the card. Some museum sites block automated access (British Museum, Brooklyn Museum, Smithsonian, MFA Boston, MoMA). For those, the check relied on the museums' API records, on files the museums uploaded themselves, or on quoted record text, and confidence is lower.

About 480 claims (title, artist, date, location, note, image) were checked; 72 works passed every check unchanged.

## Not verified (left as is)

These could not be confirmed from a reliable source. Nothing was changed.

- **Discobolus, location:** the Museo Nazionale Romano planned to move the Lancellotti copy from Palazzo Massimo to Palazzo Altemps around the end of 2026; no confirmation that it has moved.
- **Augustus of Prima Porta:** often described as a marble version of a lost bronze of c. 20 BCE; the Vatican does not say so, so the card does not.
- **Lascaux image:** the Commons file has no provenance; it may show the Lascaux II replica rather than the cave.
- **Katsina figure:** "carved from cottonwood root" is typical of katsina figures but Brooklyn lists this one only as wood; the katsina's name is spelled both Pahlikmana and Palhikmana.
- **Raven mask date:** collected in the 1880s; "19th century" kept.
- **Vézelay tympanum note:** the peoples shown include legendary ones, not only "the known world" (low confidence).
- **Birth of Venus, "life-size":** the Uffizi gives 172.5 × 278.5 cm but says nothing about life-size.
- **Wanderer above the Sea of Fog:** the Hamburger Kunsthalle captions it "um 1817"; other sources give c. 1818.
- **Paris Métro note, "for decades … style Métro":** the nickname is documented, "for decades" is not.
- **Christ the Redeemer, "the largest Art Deco sculpture":** found only in secondary sources.
- **Pollock, "sticks and hardened brushes":** secondary sources only; the Met's page does not say.
- **Höch, Cut with the Kitchen Knife:** held by the Nationalgalerie, Berlin per all secondary sources; no museum page found.
- **Metzinger, En Canot:** seized in 1937 per the Freie Universität "Degenerate Art" database as cited elsewhere; the database could not be opened.
- **Russolo:** the Pompidou's main title is "Automobile in corsa"; the card keeps the common English title "Dynamism of a Car".
- **Derain, date:** "1906" kept; the Orsay version is sometimes given as c. 1906.
- **San Vitale:** the photo shows the exterior while the note mentions the apse mosaics.
- **Venus of Willendorf:** could be dated more sharply (c. 29,500 years ago, Natural History Museum Vienna).
- **Wassily Chair photo:** shows a modern leather version.
